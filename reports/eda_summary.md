# HookSeq EDA summary

Generated by `scripts/run_eda.py` on the 50M set (K1). The stats are in `eda_stats.json` and the figures in `figures/`. Each section ends with a decision that names the real config key it sets. Preprocessing keys live at the top level of `configs/data_*.yaml`; model and training keys live in `configs/sasrec.yaml`, `configs/hookseq.yaml` and the ablations. The controller's rulings for these decisions are R23-R25.

Cleaning (`clean_report`, `hookrec.data.clean`) reconciles unlike/undislike against the like/dislike they cancel and drops them, removes duplicate rows and drops bad listens. That takes the data from 47,790,449 to 46,771,197 events: 45,879,177 listens, 790,642 likes and 101,378 dislikes. There are 9,999 users and 886,174 items.

## 1. Popularity concentration (event mix, events per user/item, power law, Gini)

- **Finding:**
  - Listens make up 98.1% of events, likes 1.7% and dislikes 0.2%.
  - Item popularity is extremely concentrated. The item Gini is 0.907. The top 1% of items take 49.2% of all events, and the top 10% take 87.9%. The power-law tail exponent is alpha ≈ 1.49.
  - Users are much less unequal, with a user Gini of 0.586.
  - Basis for `min_count` (read off the K1 item rank plot, `figures/loglog_counts.png`): roughly 3×10⁵ of the 886K items have at least 5 events, and the remaining ~590K items together carry well under 1% of all events. A cut-off of 5 therefore gives an ID row to the items behind almost all of the traffic, while keeping the ID table to about a third of the catalog.
  - These figures are approximate: they count events of any type in the whole log, while `min_count` counts positive train events. The next EDA run writes the exact numbers to `popularity.items_with_ge_k_positives` in `eda_stats.json`: items and event share with at least k positives (a listen with played_ratio_pct ≥ 50, or a like), for k ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10, 20}.
- **Figure:** `figures/event_mix.png`, `figures/loglog_counts.png`, `figures/lorenz.png`
- **Decision -> config key:**
  - `min_count: 5`: an ID embedding only for items with at least 5 positive train events. The long tail is reached through audio and artist instead.
  - `loss.logq: true` (HookSeq): sampled-softmax popularity correction. Given this concentration, in-batch negatives are heavily popularity-biased.
  - The fairness metrics' long-tail share uses this train popularity.

## 2. Played-ratio distribution (organic vs served)

- **Finding:** The played ratio is strongly bimodal.
  - Most listens are either almost complete (the 95-100% bin holds 63.3% of served and 52.2% of organic listens) or abandoned immediately (the 0-5% bin holds 18.8% of served and 25.9% of organic listens).
  - The middle bins are flat, each holding at most 1-4% of listens.
  - A 50% cut-off therefore splits the two modes cleanly. 68.3% of served listens and 59.2% of organic listens reach it.
- **Figure:** `figures/played_ratio.png`
- **Decision -> config key:** `full_threshold: 50`. A listen with `played_ratio_pct >= 50` is LISTEN_FULL, the paper's Listen+. Listen+ is the ground truth (`eval.target: listen_plus`) and the baselines' signal.

## 3. Inter-event time gaps (sessionization)

- **Finding:**
  - The log-gap histogram (38.2M gaps) has its main mode at about 100-400 s, which is one track length: consecutive listens.
  - It also has spikes at 5 s and 10 s. These are artefacts of the 5-second timestamp grid: events logged together, such as a like during a listen.
  - The automatic valley detector returns 15.9 s. That is the valley between the grid spikes and the track-length mode, not a session boundary.
  - The tail beyond about 10³ s is flat, with no second clear mode.
- **Figure:** `figures/gaps.png`
- **Decision -> config key:** `session_gap_seconds: 1800`, a top-level key of `configs/data_*.yaml` that is stored with the processed bundle.
  - The 15.9 s suggestion is rejected. It is also outside the range the gap buckets accept, which is (600, 21600) s.
  - The datasets, `SeqRecommender` and the demo read the bundle's value through `session_gap_from(cfg, data.cfg)`. A training config can override it per run with `data.session_gap_seconds`.
  - It sets the session edge of the time-gap buckets (`model.use_time_gap`, ablation a6).

## 4. Repeat consumption (re-listens, consecutive duplicates)

- **Finding:**
  - Re-consumption is the norm: 71.1% of listens are of an item the user has already listened to.
  - Immediate repeats are rare: only 2.0% of listens repeat the item of the user's previous listen.
- **Figure:** `figures/repeat.png`
- **Decision -> config key:**
  - `collapse_consecutive_dups: false`. Collapsing would remove little, and it would change the event sequence the official protocol scores.
  - Evaluation does not filter already-seen items, as in the official protocol, since re-listens are legitimate next tracks. No config key controls this.

## 5. Organic share (over time, by user activity, by item popularity)

- **Finding:**
  - About half of all events are organic. The daily share ranges from 45.5% to 59.2%, with a mean of 51.1%.
  - The share falls with user activity: 81.7% in the least active decile, 50-53% from the fourth decile up.
  - It also falls with item popularity: 73% for the least popular items, 50% for the most popular. Recommendation exposure drives the head.
- **Figure:** `figures/organic_share.png`
- **Decision -> config key:**
  - `model.use_organic: true`: an organic flag is embedded per input event in HookSeq (a2+).
  - Evaluation always reports `organic` and `served` slices of the test truth (`hookrec.eval.slices.slice_truth`). These have no config key.

## 6. Temporal drift (daily volume, new items per day, popularity churn)

- **Finding:**
  - Daily volume ranges from 95k to 239k events, with a median of 151k.
  - New items keep arriving. After the first-day warm-up, the median is 2,346 items per day and 2,246 per day over the last 30 days.
  - Popularity churn is substantial. The Jaccard distance between consecutive days' top-100 items has a median of 0.40, with a range of 0.25-0.58.
- **Figure:** `figures/daily.png`
- **Decision -> config key:**
  - The global temporal split (`split.*`, official test timestamp `split.test_timestamp`) is the right protocol under drift.
  - Recency matters for popularity: DecayPop's `decaypop.grid.half_life_days` in `configs/baselines.yaml` uses the official 0.5-24 h windows, plus 30 days (R25).

## 7. Sequence length

- **Finding:** Histories are long.
  - Events per user have a median of 2,594 and a mean of 4,678.
  - The 90th, 95th and 99th percentiles are 12,632, 16,777 and 23,752. The maximum is 27,647.
- **Figure:** `figures/seq_length.png`
- **Decision -> config key:**
  - `max_len_store: null` at 50M: the full history is stored (R23). About 47M events × 11 bytes per stored sequence set fits easily.
  - `configs/data_500m.yaml` caps it at `max_len_store: 1024`.
  - The model window stays `model.max_len: 200`, matching the official SASRec.
  - Long histories are sampled with `data.window_sampling` (R24). `last` is used for `sasrec`, giving one last window per user as in the official code. `random` is used for HookSeq and the ablations.
  - The gap buckets are computed only over the sampled window and its predecessor.
  - The baselines always use full-period aggregates, whatever the cap (R18).

## 8. Audio-embedding coverage and structure

- **Finding:**
  - 90-96% of items have an audio embedding in every popularity decile. Coverage rises from 90.4% in the least popular decile to 96.5% in the most popular.
  - The embedding map shows artist-level structure.
- **Figure:** `figures/embedding_coverage.png`, `figures/embedding_map.png`
- **Decision -> config key:**
  - `model.use_audio: true`, with `model.use_artist: true` (HookSeq a1+).
  - Items without audio use HookSeq's learned missing-audio vector plus their artist. This is built in and has no config key.
  - `pca_dim: 128` reduces the audio vectors.

## 9. Like / dislike rate vs played ratio

- **Finding:**
  - The share of listens whose (user, item) also has a like rises with played ratio, from 17-19% at low ratios to 27.6% at 80-90%. In the 90-100% bin, which holds 26.7M listens, it falls back to 19.0%.
  - The dislike rate is about 1.0-1.3% and halves to 0.6% at 90-100%.
  - Likes are therefore related to engagement but are not a substitute for Listen+.
- **Figure:** `figures/like_rate.png`
- **Decision -> config key:**
  - `model.like_head: true` with `loss.like_weight: 0.2` (ablation a5) for the multi-task like head.
  - `loss.dislike_hard_negatives: true` with `loss.n_hard: 4` (a4+): dislikes are hard negatives.
  - The ground truth excludes likes (`eval.target: listen_plus`).

## 10. Train/test overlap (cold-start magnitude)

- **Finding:**
  - 3.0% of distinct test items have no train event, and they carry 1.2% of test events.
  - The cold-item problem is small in volume but non-zero. ID-less items, those below `min_count`, add to it.
- **Figure:** `figures/cold_items.png`
- **Decision -> config key:**
  - Unknown test items stay in the truth as misses (`meta.share_unknown_test_items`, `n_oov_test_items`).
  - The `cold_items` / `warm_items` slices (`slice_truth`) report the audio path's effect on items without an ID embedding. These slices have no config key.
